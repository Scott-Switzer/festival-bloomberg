# Historical Festival Lineups and Billing Hierarchies, 1954–2001

Prepared for Scott as a quantitative and strategic research artifact.

## Executive summary

Festival billing is a market-design signal, not a single popularity ranking. A promoter assembles an artist portfolio, allocates scarce stage and time inventory, signals expected draw through graphic hierarchy, and balances cultural identity, logistics, press value, fee, agency leverage, and audience segmentation. The database must distinguish: (1) announced roster, (2) final scheduled roster, and (3) verified actual performance.

The historical arc is:

- 1950s: expert curation and reputation-market formation.
- 1960s: mass spectacle, cultural legitimacy, and discovery; slot value could create stars.
- Early 1970s: festival as community and countercultural environment, not merely concert.
- Early 1990s: touring package economics and deliberate audience segmentation.
- Late 1990s/early 2000s: destination-scale multi-stage portfolios and negotiated visual grids.

This report is conservative where the searchable evidence did not expose a primary poster/programme image. Exact typography is therefore marked as pending artifact-level extraction rather than invented.

## Evidence protocol

Evidence hierarchy: original poster/programme for printed hierarchy; contemporaneous official schedule for running order; institutional/official archive for event identity; reputable retrospective database for reconciliation; fan sources only for discovery. Every row in a production dataset should carry source URL, retrieval date, artifact version, image hash, OCR text, confidence, and whether the claim is announced, scheduled, cancelled, substituted, or performed.

## Tier definitions

Tier 1 / Headliner: festival-wide anchor, principal closer, top poster group, or strongest contemporary draw.

Tier 2 / Direct support: major draw, co-headliner, principal stage support, or late-slot act immediately below the anchor.

Tier 3 / Mid-tier: established act with meaningful draw or cultural importance but not the principal commercial anchor.

Tier 4 / Undercard: emerging, local, specialist, workshop, novelty, or early-day act. This is a market-position label, not an artistic-value judgment.

Confidence: A = official/archival and consistent; B = strong retrospective plus corroboration; C = plausible reconstruction needing artifact review; D = unresolved.

## 1. Newport Jazz Festival, 1954

Historical summaries repeatedly name Billie Holiday, Dizzy Gillespie, Ella Fitzgerald, Oscar Peterson, Gerry Mulligan, Gene Krupa, and other leading jazz figures.

Booking was a reputation portfolio: club draw, recording reputation, press prestige, booking-network access, and genre authority mattered more than later rock-era chart logic. Ensembles were the product units, often named for leaders. No surfaced evidence establishes a standardized contractual font-size rider; model programme page/block, order, and measured typography only after primary scanning.

## 2. Monterey International Pop Festival, 1967

Monterey ran June 16–18, 1967, at the Monterey County Fairgrounds. A working roster from the documentary index, historical accounts, and setlist references is:

| Tier | Artists/ensembles |
|---|---|
| 1 | The Mamas & the Papas; Otis Redding; The Who; Jefferson Airplane; Jimi Hendrix Experience |
| 2 | Simon & Garfunkel; The Byrds; Grateful Dead; Janis Joplin with Big Brother and the Holding Company; Ravi Shankar; Buffalo Springfield; Eric Burdon & the Animals |
| 3 | Hugh Masekela; Country Joe and the Fish; Paul Butterfield Blues Band; Al Kooper; Booker T. & the M.G.’s; Lou Rawls; Johnny Rivers; The Association; Laura Nyro |
| 3/4 | Beverly Martyn; Canned Heat; Steve Miller Band; Scott McKenzie; other documentary/programme acts pending artifact reconciliation |

This event’s hierarchy was strongly temporal and curatorial. Early slots could be assigned for discovery or breadth. Ravi Shankar received an unusually long, high-attention afternoon set: duration and cultural prominence were not reducible to top poster position. The Who and Hendrix illustrate breakout variance: pre-event market weight and post-event historical weight diverged. Otis Redding’s closing position is a strong Tier 1 signal.

Booking optimized cultural breadth, press value, and first-mover discovery across San Francisco rock, British invasion, soul, blues, folk, psychedelic rock, and Indian classical music. Search results also surfaced the conventional account that most artists played for charity, with Ravi Shankar receiving $3,000 for his extended sitar performance. Fee, duration, billing, and cultural importance must therefore remain separate fields.

## 3. Woodstock Music & Art Fair, 1969

The official Woodstock and Bethel Woods materials identify 32 canonical performers. The following is an analytical tiering by running-order prominence and contemporary marquee status, not retrospective fame alone.

| Tier | Performers |
|---|---|
| 1 | Richie Havens; Joan Baez; The Band; Creedence Clearwater Revival; The Who; Jefferson Airplane; Joe Cocker; Ten Years After; Crosby, Stills, Nash & Young; Jimi Hendrix |
| 2 | Arlo Guthrie; Janis Joplin; Sly & the Family Stone; Santana; Grateful Dead; Blood, Sweat & Tears |
| 3 | Sweetwater; Bert Sommer; Tim Hardin; Ravi Shankar; Country Joe McDonald; Country Joe & the Fish; Canned Heat; Mountain; Sha Na Na; John Sebastian; Melanie |
| 4 | Any remaining early/low-contemporary-draw act after official sequence and programme review |

Richie Havens opened; weather and operational delays pushed acts into unusual calendar and daylight positions; Hendrix closed the festival. Store announced order, scheduled order, actual order, delay-adjusted start, and status separately. A late closing slot can become historically definitive without proving it was the highest pre-event fee or largest expected draw.

Booking combined ticket draw, agent/manager networks, availability, counterculture credibility, genre breadth, and the need for recognizable anchors. The early poster emphasized the event proposition and “3 Days of Peace & Music” more than a modern dense artist grid. Poster versions and later programmes must not be merged into one hierarchy.

## 4. Glastonbury Fayre, 1971

The 1971 edition is pivotal for the Pyramid-stage and Glastonbury identity. Accessible archive/search evidence identifies Joan Baez, David Bowie, Traffic, Hawkwind, Fairport Convention, Family, Gong, Arthur Brown, Edgar Broughton Band, and Gilberto Gil among performers or advertised acts.

| Tier | Artists/ensembles | Status |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | David Bowie; Joan Baez | highest recognizable marquee/symbolic draw; exact printed order pending |
| 2 | Traffic; Fairport Convention; Family; Hawkwind | major British/alternative draw |
| 2/3 | Gilberto Gil; Arthur Brown; Gong | international or high-cultural-significance programming |
| 3 | Edgar Broughton Band; Pink Fairies; Skin Alley; Mighty Baby | established alternative/underground acts |
| 3/4 | folk, acoustic, community, theatre, workshop, and local acts | programme enumeration required |

Glastonbury was a mixed cultural programme. Workshops, ritual, theatre, food, film, and community/free components affected the meaning of stage time. These should be separate act types, not forced into a commercial rank. Selection depended on countercultural legitimacy, personal networks, genre fit, ideological alignment, and ability to help create a temporary community.

For the 1971 poster/booklet, identify artifact type and production date before interpreting hierarchy. Compare OCR/layout emphasis against the film and programme sequence. Do not infer a font-size rider from visual prominence without contract, promoter, agent, or contemporary interview evidence.

## 5. Lollapalooza, 1991

The core 1991 touring sequence surfaced consistently as Jane’s Addiction; Siouxsie and the Banshees; Living Colour; Nine Inch Nails; Ice-T & Body Count; Butthole Surfers; Rollins Band; Violent Femmes. Venue-specific attractions may differ.

| Tier | Artist/ensemble | Billing logic |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Jane’s Addiction | package anchor and farewell-context headliner |
| 2 | Siouxsie and the Banshees; Living Colour; Nine Inch Nails | high-profile international/national support and audience anchors |
| 3 | Ice-T & Body Count; Butthole Surfers | strong genre/cultural draw; audience expansion |
| 4 | Rollins Band; Violent Femmes | specialist alternative value, lower package hierarchy |
| Side/associated | Perry Farrell’s travelling circus, visual/dance/activist attractions, venue-specific additions | separate from core music bill |

Lollapalooza changed the unit of analysis from one-off festival to travelling package. Every act had to work across markets; the package supplied the brand. Support meant differentiated audience segment and scene credibility, not simply second-place popularity. Genre adjacency, controversy, authenticity, and the ability to make an alternative package feel larger than an arena concert mattered.

The Rock Hall catalogue record identifies a June 17, 1991 press release and poster for an August 5 concert at Blossom Music Center. This is a particularly valuable archival object for measuring actual poster hierarchy rather than relying on retrospective lists.

## 6. Coachella Valley Music and Arts Festival, 1999

The inaugural Coachella edition was held at the Empire Polo Club in October 1999. Search evidence identifies Beck, Rage Against the Machine, and Tool as headliner-level anchors. Historical lineup references also associate the event with The Chemical Brothers, Morrissey, Underworld, Ben Harper, Toad the Wet Sprocket, Jurassic 5, and a wider electronic/alternative/hip-hop supporting field. The headline layer is reliable; complete side-stage extraction requires programme-image reconciliation.

| Tier | Artists/ensembles | Status/rationale |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Beck; Rage Against the Machine; Tool | cross-genre destination draws and strongest inaugural anchors |
| 2 | The Chemical Brothers; Morrissey; Underworld | major international alternative/electronic draws |
| 2/3 | Ben Harper; Toad the Wet Sprocket; Jurassic 5 | established or high-momentum national/regional draw |
| 3 | electronic, alternative, indie, and hip-hop acts in programme references | exact poster rank pending |
| 4 | emerging DJs, local acts, side-stage performers | never compare raw rank with main-stage acts without context normalization |

Coachella was a destination-market experiment. It needed national/regional anchors to justify travel to Indio, while differentiating itself from arena tours through environment and genre portfolio. Booking criteria included touring availability, label/agent relationships, regional draw, genre complementarity, and electronic credibility. Parallel schedules made stage allocation a product-design variable: they created choice, controlled crowd flow, and monetized taste clusters.

By 1999 the lineup poster was an industry status ledger. Relative type size and grid placement signalled value to fans, agents, labels, and competing festivals. Record poster version, release date, font dimensions, position, stage/day, additions, and moves.

## Cross-era booking and billing analysis

### Selection variables

1950s: club draw, recording reputation, press prestige, leader status, regional access, and genre authority.

1960s: current draw plus cultural legitimacy, scene representation, first-mover discovery, documentary/press value, and diversity.

Early 1970s: trust networks, ideological fit, counterculture credibility, community-building, and willingness to participate in a nonstandard environment.

1991: touring-package economics, cross-market viability, differentiated fan segments, controversy, scene authenticity, and package-level brand fit.

1999–2001: destination draw, stage portfolio balance, international availability, electronic/genre anchors, agency leverage, repeatability, and ticket-price/attendance experimentation.

### Why billing moved from lists to visual negotiation

Small early programmes could use marquee blocks and order-of-appearance. As bills expanded, the promoter needed a compact information architecture. By the 1990s the poster did four jobs: consumer information, expectation management, festival identity, and a public record of negotiated status. Agents could seek “top line,” “first line,” “not smaller than,” “not less prominent than,” co-headline, “with,” or special-guest language. Exact contractual enforcement varies and should be recorded only when documented.

The shift was not simply alphabetical to ranked. It was from one-dimensional ordering to a multi-axis grid: festival-wide prominence, day, stage, stage-closing position, visual area, typography, and contextual genre role. Multi-stage festivals made raw poster rank especially unreliable unless the context is stage/day normalized.

## Quantitative framework

For artist i, edition e, context c (festival poster, day, stage, or programme):

Printed rank: P = 1 - (rank - 1) / max(1, N - 1).

Tier prominence: T = 1 - (tier - 1) / max(1, number_of_tiers - 1).

Schedule prominence: S = .40 close_stage + .25 close_day + .20 stage_capacity_percentile + .15 duration_percentile.

Visual prominence: V = .40 robust-normalized log text area + .25 font height + .15 font weight + .10 contrast/color prominence + .10 logo/lockup area.

Evidence confidence: G = 1 - product over independent claims j of (1 - q_j), where q is reliability times extraction confidence. Copied pages must not count as independent.

Explainable Festival Billing Score:

FBS(i,e) = 100 x G(i,e) x [0.35P + 0.25T + 0.20S + 0.20V].

Renormalize weights for missing dimensions and publish a completeness vector. The result is not an objective “importance” score; it is a reproducible estimate of observed billing prominence.

Career trajectory with five-year half-life:

Trajectory(i,y) = sum(w(e,y) x FBS(i,e)) / sum(w(e,y)), where w(e,y) = exp[-ln(2)(y-year_e)/5].

Report rolling score, robust slope over three/five/ten years, volatility, promotion rate, cross-festival breadth, maximum observed tier, and bootstrap uncertainty. Compare within festival family, genre, geography, and edition size to avoid treating an archival bias as a career effect. For prediction, fit an ordinal mixed-effects model to tier or placement percentile using only pre-event features; do not leak retrospective fame.

## Rights and source cautions

Respect robots.txt, terms, rate limits, copyright, and item-level archive rights. Store metadata, URLs, hashes, OCR, and derived measurements rather than redistributing scans unless permitted. Songkick’s API is credential/terms dependent; community sources such as Concert Archives are useful leads but not automatically open bulk data. Treat Wikipedia and fan lists as discovery layers, not sole evidence for billing scores.
